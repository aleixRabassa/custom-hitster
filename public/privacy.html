Privacy Policy

This policy covers Playlist Jitster, both the website and the Android app, which are the same application.

Who runs this

Playlist Jitster is made and run by Aleix Rabassa as a personal project. Contact: aleixrabassa.vercel.app.

The short version

There are no accounts, so the app never asks who you are. It stores your game on your own device and nowhere else. It sends a playlist link and, for each track, a title, artist and duration to its own server so it can look up release years. It has no advertising, no analytics and no third-party tracking code of any kind.

What stays on your device

The app uses your browser's localStorage under exactly two keys, and neither is ever transmitted anywhere:

  • hitster:session:v1 — the game in progress: the playlists it was dealt from, the shuffled deck with the years already resolved, and which card you are on. It lets you reload or come back later and carry on.
  • hitster:library:v1 — playlists you chose to keep, stored as a name and a list of Spotify playlist ids so they can be offered again on the start screen.

Both are written and read by your browser only. There is no account, no sync and no server copy of either. Deck PDFs and share links are also produced entirely on your device: a share link is just playlist ids plus a shuffle seed placed on your clipboard, and a printable deck is generated in the browser and never uploaded.

What our server receives

The app calls two endpoints of its own. They hold no database of users and store nothing tied to you.

  • Reading a playlist. When you start a game, the playlist link you pasted is sent to our server, which reduces it to the playlist id and fetches Spotify's public embed page for that playlist to get the track list. This request is made by our server, so Spotify sees the server rather than you. The track list is returned to your device; we keep no copy of it, though our hosting provider's CDN may hold the response for a few minutes so a repeated request is not fetched twice.
  • Looking up a year. For each track, the app sends the track's title, artist and duration to our server, which queries the public MusicBrainz database for the original release year. MusicBrainz is contacted by our server, so it does not see you either.

Year answers are cached so the same track is not looked up repeatedly for everyone. The cache entry is keyed on the artist and title alone, holds the year, how confident the lookup was and a few technical notes about how it was found, and expires by itself: 30 days for a confident answer, 7 days for a weaker one, 1 day when no year was found. It runs on Upstash Redis. The same answers are also held by our hosting provider's CDN for similar periods. Nothing in either cache identifies a person, a device or a playlist — a cached year is indistinguishable from one looked up by anybody else.

What Spotify receives directly

One thing only: the 30-second preview clip. When you press Play, your browser loads the audio straight from Spotify's servers, so Spotify receives your IP address and the usual request details for that file, under its own privacy policy. If you never press Play, your device never contacts Spotify.

The QR code on a card is generated on your device — nothing is sent to produce it. It encodes a public Spotify track link, so scanning it opens Spotify on whichever device did the scanning, and that device's use of Spotify is covered by Spotify's policy, not this one.

Hosting

The site and its two endpoints are hosted on Vercel. As with any website, requests from your device reach the host, which processes ordinary connection metadata such as your IP address and browser user agent in order to serve the request and to protect the service from abuse. The app itself does not read, use or retain that metadata.

What the app does not do

  • No accounts, sign-in, email addresses or profiles.
  • No advertising and no ad networks.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage measurement.
  • No third-party SDKs, tag managers, social widgets or embedded trackers.
  • No cookies are set by the app.
  • No selling or sharing of data, because there is no data about you to sell or share.
  • Nothing is knowingly collected from children, or from anyone else.

The Android app

The Android app is a thin shell around the same website. It contains no code of its own that collects information, includes no Google Play services or advertising libraries, requests no notification permission and asks for no device permissions such as location, contacts, camera, microphone or storage. Everything described above applies to it unchanged.

The printable year cards

The year-cards PDF offered on the welcome screen is a fixed file served from this site. The download sends nothing about you beyond the ordinary request for the file described under Hosting.

Deleting your data

Everything the app keeps is on your device. Clearing this site's data in your browser — on Android, in Chrome, since the app runs inside it — removes both storage keys and leaves nothing behind. There is no server-side record tied to you, so there is nothing to request the deletion of.
